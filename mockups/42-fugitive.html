Fugitive

The archive records the pigment for fifty-four colours in the studio, with its Colour Index name and its lightfastness. Which means the archive already knows, and can tell you, which of these paintings will outlive the painter and which will not.

What the rating meansISO 105-B02

Blue Wool is a permanence scale. A pigment rated 8 is essentially immortal on a wall: two centuries of ordinary gallery light will not move it. A pigment rated 1 is fugitive — it will visibly shift inside a human lifetime, and in direct sun inside a decade.

Artists have always known this and mostly not said it out loud, because it sounds like an admission. It isn't. A rose that fades is a choice, the same as a paper that yellows or a bronze that goes green. The only failure is not telling the person who bought it.

Simulated fade · stole the rainbow, 2021hand-coloured, watercolour & gouache

This is a simulation, not a measurement. The archive records which pigments are in the studio; it does not yet record which pigment went into which work. These four frames apply a plausible desaturation curve to the photograph — they show you the shape of the risk, not this work's fate. Filling in per-work pigment data would turn this from an illustration into a forecast, and it is the single most valuable thing the catalogue is still missing.

The same again · low angle sun rays, 2024oil on linen

Oil on linen behaves differently from watercolour on rag: the binder yellows as the pigment shifts, so the whole thing warms rather than simply pales. That is why the two rows above do not fade the same way.
