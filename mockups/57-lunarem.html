Cyclum Lunarem · twenty-eight days · one work each

Lunarem

In 2017 he painted the moons from an entire summer, starting on the summer solstice and completed on the autumnal equinox — each moon painted upon a 3⅜″ square prepared with linen and a traditional gesso ground, gilded with 24kt gold leaf. This is that structure turned into a way of moving through everything else — twenty-eight nights, and the work only visible where the moon is lit.

Why a lunar month

Because a website that changes four times a year is a magazine, and a website that changes every night is a practice. Twenty-eight is a small enough number that somebody could actually see all of it, and a long enough cycle that coming back is worth it.

How the works were chosen

By measured luminance. The darkest work in the catalogue sits at new moon; the lightest at full; everything else is placed on the waxing and waning limbs by where its own light falls. No editorial hand at all — which is either the joke or the point.

What it would take

Nothing. The phase is arithmetic and the luminance is already stored. A real version would compute today's phase, open on that panel, and be a different front page every night for a month without anybody composing anything.
